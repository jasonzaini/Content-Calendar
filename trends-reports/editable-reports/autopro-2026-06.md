# AutoPro — June 2026

Historical social intelligence | Automotive servicing, maintenance and car wash

## Executive brief

Lead with roadworthiness and useful explanations

- Prioritise technician-led tyre and vehicle-check education.

- Acknowledge cost concerns without promising unproven fuel savings.

- Use source-reviewed safety guidance and approved service claims.

### Brand interpretation

Police safety guidance and qualitative driving-cost coverage create a direct education opportunity. Explain what a technician checks, what a driver can observe and when professional inspection is appropriate. Avoid fear-led scripts, invented crash statistics, universal tyre-pressure numbers or claims that a service guarantees lower fuel use.

## Proposed content briefs

### What the technician checks first

Hook: Before the next drive, start here.

A technician demonstrates one authorised tyre inspection step, points to a real condition and explains the next action. Technical and safety review is mandatory.

Format: Reel / Short | CTA: Ask the team about a check.

Production / checks: Qualified technical review; actual vehicle/service scope and disclosures. Customer or technician permissions; no unsafe demonstration.

### A warning sign, explained

Hook: What does this sign actually mean?

Use a safe stationary vehicle and one approved warning example. Explain when to stop safely or seek qualified help; never diagnose every car from a generic clip.

Format: Carousel | CTA: Save this explanation.

Production / checks: Qualified technical review; actual vehicle/service scope and disclosures. Customer or technician permissions; no unsafe demonstration.

### Care without the fuel promise

Hook: Maintain it well. Understand it better.

Show the service process and genuine customer questions about running costs. Describe the actual service scope without claiming a percentage improvement.

Format: Reel | CTA: What would you ask a technician?

Production / checks: Qualified technical review; actual vehicle/service scope and disclosures. Customer or technician permissions; no unsafe demonstration.
